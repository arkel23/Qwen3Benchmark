One recent direction addressing these constraints is the SLAM-ASR architecture \citep{maEmbarrassinglySimpleApproach2024}. It connects a pretrained speech encoder to a large language model (LLM) through a trainable linear projector. Both pretrained models stay frozen. Follow-up work has probed the recipe's robustness \citep{kumarPerformanceEvaluationSLAMASR2025} and its data requirements \citep{fongSpeechLLMsLowResource2025}. It has also probed encoder depth \citep{kolluriRoleEncoderDepth2026}, language-family effects \citep{zhangLanguageFamilyMatters2026}, and prompt sensitivity \citep{burdissoReducingPromptSensitivity2026}. However, the effect of the decoder's \emph{regional specialization} has not been studied. The Tiny Aya family \citep{salamancaTinyAyaBridging2026} makes the missing experiment possible. Its small multilingual LLMs share a common base model and tokenizer. Each regional variant differs only in the post-training data reinforcing its region's languages.
