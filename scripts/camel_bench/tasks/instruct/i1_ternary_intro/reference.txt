A speech large language model (LLM) for recognition usually has three parts \cite{ma_embarrassingly_2024,chu2024qwen2audio}. These are a speech encoder, a text LLM decoder, and a projector that maps encoder frames into the decoder's embedding space. The decoder holds most of the parameters and most of the memory traffic at decode time. It is therefore the part worth compressing. Ternary LLMs store every linear weight as one of three values \cite{ma2024bitnet158,wang2023bitnet}. Open ternary decoders now exist at useful sizes. Falcon-E was trained with quantization-aware training \cite{falconllm2025falcone}. Ternary-Bonsai was distilled from Qwen3 \cite{prismml2026bonsaiternary,yang_qwen3_2025}. No published result places one of them behind a speech encoder.
